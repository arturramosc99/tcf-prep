# Correction method

How to correct a TCF Canada Expression écrite simulation. The order matters.

## 1. Word count
Compare each text with its limit (see the task structure). The app counts words by splitting on spaces and
gives you the numbers. Going over or under the limit weighs more than any grammar mistake: say so first.

**Tâche 3:** check Partie 1 (40–60) and Partie 2 (80–120) separately, and give the total too. One part inside
its limit does not make up for the other part outside it.

## 2. Task fulfilment (before grammar)
Check against the prompt, in this order:
- Is every element the prompt asks for covered with real content? If it asks for a place, a means of
  transport and activities, all three must be there, not just mentioned.
- Do the verb tenses match the prompt's timeline (already happened / will happen / hypothetical)?
- Is the register (tu/vous, formal/informal) right for the relationship described, and kept throughout?
- If the prompt asks for two sides (difficulties and positives, for and against), are both developed, or is
  one squeezed into a last sentence?

**Extra checks for Tâche 3:**
- Partie 1 presents the topic and summarises each document, with no personal opinion, in the candidate's own
  words (no copied sentences). Both documents get balanced space.
- Partie 2 gives an opinion, a nuance, a possible solution or compromise, and a conclusion. The nuance and the
  solution say something of their own, linked to the documents' arguments.
- The opinion, the solution and the conclusion don't contradict each other.

If something here fails, say it first and clearly. It is what weighs most in the real score.

## 3. Errors — grouped by pattern, not by order of appearance
- Check the list of patterns already recorded for this candidate. If an error belongs to one, say how many
  times it has appeared and treat it as a high priority, not as a new finding.
- Group errors of the same kind under one explanation (all gender errors together, not scattered).
- For each error: the wrong form → the right form → the rule in one sentence. No long grammar lessons.
- Say what worked well in one sentence, with no empty praise: name what worked and why it matters.

## 4. Corrected version
Give the full corrected text, keeping the candidate's content and ideas. Only the language changes; never
rewrite their content from scratch.

## Tone
- Direct, no filler, no generic validation.
- Say plainly when an error is serious (it breaks task fulfilment, or it is the fifth or sixth repeat).
- Never invent a rule. When unsure about a fine point, say so instead of guessing.
