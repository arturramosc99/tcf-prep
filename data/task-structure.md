# Expression écrite — task structure (TCF Canada)

Three tâches in 60 minutes. The candidate manages their own time and can move between tâches.
Word limits are strict: a text outside its limit is penalised.

---

## Tâche 1 — Short message (60–120 words)
A message to a friend, a group or someone the candidate doesn't know (invitation, reply to an ad,
information, thanks, request).

**Expected shape**
1. Greeting suited to the relationship (*Salut Léa,* / *Bonjour à tous,* / *Madame, Monsieur,*).
2. Why they are writing, in one sentence.
3. The key details the prompt asks for (date, place, price, directions, what to bring…).
4. A closing line that asks for an answer or offers help.
5. A sign-off suited to the register (*À bientôt,* / *Bien à vous,* / *Cordialement,*) and a name.

**What to check:** every requested detail is there; tu or vous is consistent with the relationship;
the message reads as a real message, not a list.

---

## Tâche 2 — Personal experience + opinion (120–150 words)
The candidate tells about something they lived (a trip, an event, a new job, a move, a course…), usually for
a blog, a newspaper or a friend, and gives their opinion of it.

**Expected shape**
1. Introduction: what the experience was, when and where.
2. How it went: the main events, in a clear order, in past tenses (passé composé for events, imparfait for
   description and background).
3. A highlight or a disappointment.
4. Their opinion and what they learnt.
5. A recommendation (or a warning) and a short conclusion.

**What to check:** past tenses are used correctly; the story and the opinion are both developed; a negative
experience is allowed as long as the prompt doesn't ask for a positive one.

---

## Tâche 3 — Summary of two documents + argued opinion
**Partie 1: 40–60 words. Partie 2: 80–120 words.** Each part has its own limit.

The prompt gives a title (the debate) and two short documents with opposite or complementary views.

**Partie 1 — present the debate and summarise both documents, with no opinion.**
A good Partie 1 has three moves: introduce the topic, summarise document 1, summarise document 2 — in the
candidate's own words, with a contrast connector between the two (*en revanche*, *à l'inverse*,
*au contraire*).

**Partie 2 — argued opinion.**
1. A clear position (*Selon moi…*, *Je suis plutôt d'avis que…*).
2. A nuance or counter-argument (*Toutefois…*, *Il faut néanmoins reconnaître que…*).
3. A solution or compromise (*Une solution pourrait être de…*).
4. A conclusion consistent with the position (*En somme…*, *En définitive…*).

**What to check:** no opinion in Partie 1; no copied sentences from the documents; both documents covered in a
balanced way; the four moves of Partie 2 each have real content; no contradiction between position and
conclusion.

---

## Useful connectors (for the corrector's B2+ rewrites)
- Adding: *de plus*, *en outre*, *par ailleurs*
- Contrast: *cependant*, *pourtant*, *en revanche*, *alors que*
- Cause and consequence: *puisque*, *étant donné que*, *c'est pourquoi*, *si bien que*
- Concession: *bien que* + subjonctif, *même si*, *certes… mais*
- Concluding: *en somme*, *pour conclure*, *en définitive*
